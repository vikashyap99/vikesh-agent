  His approach recognizes an important distinction: an AI model can be capable of reasoning about code and still produce a poor answer if it does not have the right information.
  Context engineering and token optimization
  Vikash has been exploring context engineering: the practice of designing and organizing the information supplied to AI systems so they can reason more effectively.
  One of the ideas he has been developing is the use of a context graph instead of repeatedly stuffing an entire codebase into a prompt.
  For example, imagine asking an AI agent, "Where does this functionality live?" Providing the entire repository may consume a large number of tokens and still leave the model struggling to identify the relevant implementation.
  A better approach is to help the agent navigate relationships between modules, components, APIs, configuration, and dependencies. The agent can then retrieve the specific context it needs rather than repeatedly processing everything.
  This line of thinking connects several of his interests:
- Reducing unnecessary model input and inference costs.
- Improving the relevance and accuracy of AI-generated answers.
- Helping agents understand unfamiliar codebases.
- Designing better retrieval and navigation workflows.
- Making AI-assisted development more predictable and maintainable.
  He has also worked on communicating these ideas through technical writing. In October 2026, he began developing a Medium article and a related LinkedIn post about moving from stuffing codebases into prompts toward building context graphs.
  For Vikash, this is both a practical engineering concern and a broader question about how developers should build software in an AI-assisted world.
  Agentic AI and multi-agent systems
  Vikash's interests extend beyond individual coding assistants to agentic AI.
  He has explored ideas around the OpenAI SDK and agent systems involving tools, guardrails, and multi-agent orchestration. He is interested in the mechanisms that allow an AI system to do more than generate a single response.
  These include:
- Planning a task and breaking it into smaller steps.
- Choosing and invoking tools.
- Maintaining state across multiple actions.
- Coordinating specialized agents.
- Handling failures and deciding when to retry.
- Evaluating results rather than assuming the first output is correct.
- Managing long-running workflows and the context they require.
- Keeping tool usage controlled and outputs useful.
  He is particularly interested in the difference between an impressive demonstration and a system that can operate reliably in production.
  This is a natural extension of his engineering experience. The same principles that matter in traditional software—state management, error handling, observability, clear interfaces, and predictable behavior—also matter when building systems around AI models.
  Specification-driven and AI-assisted development
  Another area of interest is how to make AI-assisted development more structured.
  Rather than giving a coding agent a vague instruction and accepting whatever it generates, Vikash has explored providing detailed product requirements, design tokens, architectural context, and other supporting information.
  The goal is to reduce ambiguity before implementation begins.
  He is also interested in using AI to accelerate development without abandoning engineering discipline. For example, profiling an application before asking an AI agent to refactor performance-sensitive code gives the agent a concrete problem to address instead of encouraging speculative changes.
  This reflects a broader principle in his work: AI can increase the speed of execution, but good context, verification, and engineering judgment determine whether the result is actually good.

Why he decided to build Zestro
The motivation came from a real business problem rather than a purely technical exercise.
Through owning Tattva Cafe, Vikash could see the practical challenges restaurant operators face. Running a restaurant involves more than preparing food and serving customers.
